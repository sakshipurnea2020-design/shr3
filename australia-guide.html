Section 1

Why Choose Australia?

Australia is a popular destination for international students because of its English-medium education, internationally recognised universities, research opportunities, and access to a range of professional industries. The country offers programmes in business, engineering, information technology, healthcare, social sciences, law, and research.

Important planning note: Studying in Australia requires careful financial planning. Tuition fees, accommodation, health insurance, and visa charges can be substantial. Immigration rules have also become more restrictive in several areas — assess costs and career prospects before committing to a programme.
Section 2

Australia's Higher Education System

Australia has public universities, private higher education providers, vocational education institutions, and specialised colleges.

Bachelor's degrees: Undergraduate programmes generally take three to four years.
Master's degrees: Most coursework-based master's programmes take one to two years, depending on the qualification and previous study.
Doctoral degrees: PhD programmes commonly take three to four years of full-time study.
Vocational education: Technical and vocational courses provide practical training for particular occupations and industries.
Important: Students should confirm that their chosen institution and course are registered to enrol international students. Registration is important for student visa eligibility.

Section 4

Admission Requirements

Admission requirements depend on the institution, course, and level of study. For postgraduate programmes, universities commonly require:

A recognised bachelor's degree with academic grades meeting programme entry requirements
English language proficiency evidence: IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT, or another accepted test
Academic transcripts and degree certificates
A statement of purpose or personal statement, where required
Letters of recommendation, a CV, portfolio, or relevant work experience for selected programmes
For Indian students: Check the entry requirements specifically for applicants with Indian qualifications. Requirements can differ significantly between universities and disciplines. Research master's degrees and PhD programmes may also require a research proposal and supervisor agreement.

Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485)

Eligible international graduates may be able to apply for the Temporary Graduate visa, subclass 485. Under the Australia-India Economic Cooperation and Trade Agreement (AI-ECTA), eligible Indian nationals may receive longer post-study stays:

Bachelor's degree: Up to two years
Bachelor's with first-class honours in STEM including ICT: Up to three years
Master's degree: Up to three years
Doctoral degree: Up to four years
Important: These periods are subject to applicable visa stream, eligibility criteria, and immigration rules. Not every course or graduate automatically qualifies. Check age limits, English requirements, health insurance, and the current application charge before relying on post-study work for financial plans.
🔗 Official Subclass 485 Guidance — immi.homeaffairs.gov.au
Section 8

Student Visa Requirements (Subclass 500)

International students generally need a Student visa, subclass 500, to study in Australia. The process usually involves:

Receiving an offer from an eligible education provider
Obtaining a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)
Meeting the applicable English language requirements
Providing evidence of sufficient funds for tuition, living expenses, and travel
Arranging the required Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)
Meeting health, character, and other visa requirements
Visa fee increase: From 1 July 2026, the published base charge for the main applicant is AUD 2,500, although charges and exemptions depend on the application. Check the official fee schedule before applying.
🔗 Official Student Visa (Subclass 500) Guidance
Section 9

Part-Time Work During Studies

Eligible international students can generally work up to 48 hours per fortnight while their course is in session, subject to the conditions of their student visa. Different rules may apply during scheduled course breaks and to certain research students.

Planning note: Part-time employment can help with everyday expenses, but students should not depend on finding a job immediately after arrival or assume that employment income will cover full tuition and living costs. Always follow visa conditions and balance work with academic requirements.
Section 10

Employment Prospects After Graduation

Australia has employment opportunities across healthcare, professional services, engineering, technology, education, and other sectors. Health Care and Social Assistance is Australia's largest employing industry, with strong projected growth in healthcare and professional services.

Realistic expectation: This does not mean every graduate will find a suitable job. Some occupations require professional registration, local experience, or additional qualifications. Employers may also prefer candidates with relevant work experience. A university degree and a temporary graduate visa do not guarantee employment or permanent residence.
Section 11

Permanent Residency and Skilled Migration

Australia has several migration pathways including points-tested skilled visas, state or territory nomination, and employer-sponsored options. Eligibility depends on age, English proficiency, qualifications, relevant work experience, occupation, skills assessment, and nomination or sponsorship requirements.

Important: Meeting the minimum points threshold does not guarantee an invitation to apply. Indian graduates should treat permanent residency as a possible future outcome rather than a guaranteed result of studying in Australia. Invitation outcomes vary by occupation and migration programme — state nomination settings can change.

MATES Scheme

Indian graduates may also wish to investigate the Mobility Arrangement for Talented Early-professionals Scheme (MATES) — a separate work opportunity for eligible Indian graduates and early-career professionals in specified fields. It is not a student visa route, and eligibility depends on the scheme's current rules.

🔗 Official Immigration Information — immi.homeaffairs.gov.au

Indian students may benefit from specific post-study arrangements under AI-ECTA. Always consult official university websites and the Australian Government's Department of Home Affairs before applying.
