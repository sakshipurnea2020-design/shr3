📋 The Tests

Which English Language Test Do You Need?

There is no single English test suitable for every student. Universities may accept different examinations, and requirements vary by country, course and level of study.

📝
Most Widely Accepted

IELTS

International English Language Testing System — one of the world's most recognised examinations. IELTS Academic is commonly used for higher-education applications.

  • Listening
  • Reading
  • Writing
  • Speaking
💻
Computer-Based

PTE Academic

Pearson Test of English Academic — a fully computer-based exam assessing academic English proficiency. Accepted by many universities worldwide.

  • Speaking
  • Writing
  • Reading
  • Listening
🎓
Internationally Recognised

TOEFL iBT

Test of English as a Foreign Language — measures ability to use and understand English in an academic environment, used by universities globally.

  • Reading
  • Listening
  • Speaking
  • Writing
🌐
Online & Convenient

Duolingo English Test

An online English proficiency exam that can be taken remotely. Accepted by many universities, though acceptance varies — always verify with your chosen institution.

  • Online
  • Remote
  • Fast Results
🏆
Advanced Level

Cambridge English

Qualifications such as C1 Advanced and C2 Proficiency assess advanced English skills. Recognised by universities, employers and organisations across multiple countries.

  • C1 Advanced
  • C2 Proficiency
  • Globally Recognised
🏥
Healthcare Professionals

OET

Occupational English Test — designed specifically for healthcare professionals. Assesses English communication in healthcare contexts for doctors, nurses and allied health workers.

  • Healthcare Specific
  • Professional Use
  • Overseas Registration

🎯 How to Choose

How Do You Choose the Right English Test?

Choosing a test should be based on your specific university and course requirements — not simply the most popular exam. Consider these factors before registering.

1

Your Destination

Different countries and institutions may have different English-language requirements.

2

Your University

Check which English tests your specific university accepts for your chosen course.

3

Your Course

Some programmes may have higher or additional English-language requirements.

4

Your Required Score

Universities may specify both an overall score and minimum scores in individual components.

5

Your Application Deadline

Allow enough time for preparation, testing and receiving results before your deadline.

6

Visa or Professional Requirements

In some cases, visa or professional registration requirements may differ from admission requirements.

English Test Requirements

English-language requirements can vary significantly. A university may accept several different tests while requiring different minimum scores for each.

🎁 BONUS GUIDE

How to Prepare for IELTS in 30 Days

Need to take IELTS soon? If you already have a decent level of English, you don't necessarily need months of preparation — what you need is a proper plan. Take a test, find your weak areas, work on them, practise again.

Week 1 (Days 2–7): Understand Each Skill

📖 Reading

  • True / False / Not Given
  • Matching Headings
  • Multiple Choice
  • Sentence Completion
  • Summary Completion
  • Matching Information

🎧 Listening

  • Follow the conversation closely
  • Watch out for spelling
  • Numbers, dates and names
  • Singular and plural answers
  • Word limit on answers

✍️ Writing

  • Task 1: Describe information clearly
  • Task 2: Answer the question properly
  • Focus on structure, not complexity
  • Simple, well-organised essay wins

🗣️ Speaking

  • Record yourself speaking
  • Part 2: speak for two minutes
  • Notice pauses, repeated words
  • Develop thoughts, not scripts
Your 30-Day Progress 0% Complete
DAY
1

Take a Full Mock Test

Before you start preparing, find out where you actually stand. Take a complete IELTS Academic mock test under proper exam conditions — no pausing, no Google, keep to time limits.

  • Don't pause the listening section
  • Complete both writing tasks in full
  • Record yourself for the speaking section
  • Analyse each section separately after
DAYS
2–7

Understand the Exam Format

The first week is about getting comfortable with the IELTS format. Don't worry about completing full tests every day — start with individual question types.

  • Work through each question type individually
  • When you get an answer wrong, find the exact part of the passage that contains the answer
  • Ask yourself why you got it wrong — that's more useful than simply checking the answer
DAYS
8–14

Work on Your Weak Areas

Now you know what is costing you marks. Your preparation should become targeted — spend more time where you need improvement, not where you're already strong.

  • If Reading is strong, don't spend 3 hours a day on it
  • Learn useful topic vocabulary you can actually use correctly
  • Don't try to memorise 1,000 new words — learn phrases you can use naturally
  • Practise words in context: access to education, tuition fees, career opportunities
DAYS
15–21

Start Working with the Clock

By week three, practise under proper time limits. Knowing the answers is not enough — you need to complete the test within the given time.

  • Reading: roughly 20 minutes per passage
  • Writing: 20 min Task 1, 40 min Task 2
  • Listening: do the full test once without stopping, then review
  • Speaking: develop thoughts, not memorised answers
DAYS
22–27

Full Mock Tests — With Review

Do complete tests now. But there is one rule: don't just check your score and move on. After every mock test, write down exactly what went wrong.

  • Don't just note the score — note what caused you to lose marks
  • Your score tells you where you are. Your mistakes tell you what to fix.
  • Example: "I lost marks on Matching Headings and spent too long on Passage 3"
DAYS
28–29

Revise Your Common Mistakes

By now you should have a list of mistakes you keep making. Go through it carefully and focus only on the things that have repeatedly caused problems.

  • Grammar and spelling mistakes
  • Reading question types you struggle with
  • Listening errors and vocabulary misuse
  • Speaking habits — repeated words, long pauses
DAY
30

Keep It Simple

The day before your test is not the time to panic. Do a light revision. Check your test details. If taking the test online, check your equipment and surroundings. Get enough sleep. You have already done the preparation.

  • Confirm test location and time
  • Check equipment if taking online
  • Light revision only — no new material
  • Get a good night's sleep

A Simple 2-Hour Daily Plan

🎧 Listening30 minutes
📖 Reading30 minutes
✍️ Writing40 minutes
🗣️ Speaking20 minutes

If you have more time, spend it reviewing mistakes rather than endlessly taking another practice test. If you only have one hour, alternate: Day 1 Reading + Writing → Day 2 Listening + Speaking → repeat.

5 Things to Remember

1

Take a mock test before you start. You need to know your starting point before you can plan effectively.

2

Don't just practise — review. A practice test is useful only if you understand your mistakes. The review is where you actually learn.

3

Don't memorise essays. Learn how to structure and develop your own answers. Examiners can tell when something has been memorised.

4

Don't force complicated vocabulary. Correct and natural English is more useful than difficult words you are not comfortable using.

5

Practise with a timer. Knowing the answers is not enough. You also need to complete the test within the given time.

Can You Really Prepare for IELTS in 30 Days?

It depends on your current level of English and the score you need. If you already communicate comfortably in English, 30 days of focused preparation can give you enough time to understand the exam, work on your weak areas and get used to the format.

If you are starting with a much lower level of English, you may need longer. There is no genuine shortcut to improving your English — but there is a difference between studying for IELTS and simply studying English. If your English is already good, your preparation should focus on understanding the test, improving your technique and avoiding the mistakes that are costing you marks.

Start with a mock test today. Find out where you stand. Then use the next 30 days properly.
